The problem you already know

Three moments every project hits — and nobody has a system for.

Pick one. Or pick all three. If you've worked on a build of any size, at least one of these is sitting on your desk right now.

01 · The Change Order

"That was approved on the call last Tuesday."

The contractor swears the Owner verbally agreed. The Architect remembers it differently. The ORPM has it as "discussed, not approved." There's no timestamp, no sequence, no record of who said what. Six weeks later, $180K is in dispute and the steel is already in the ground.

02 · The Pay Application

"It's been with the Owner for eleven days."

The contractor submitted Pay App #7 on the 1st. Architect certified it. ORPM recommended it. Owner is "reviewing." Eleven days in, the GC is carrying payroll on a line of credit and the response window in the contract is gone. Nobody can tell you exactly when it expired or what that expiration costs.

03 · The Dispute

"Pull every document for discovery."

Eighteen months in, somebody files. The lawyers want the full record — every redline, every RFI, every approval, every email that touched the change. What they get is a Dropbox, three Outlook archives, and a project manager's memory. The reconstruction takes ninety days and costs more than the dispute.

These aren't bad-faith problems. They're recordkeeping problems — and they happen because the contract sits in a binder while the project lives in calendars, email, drawings, change directives, and pay app spreadsheets. The language exists. The meaning gets lost in execution.

How KPAR Works

It takes the contract you already have and makes it computable.

KPAR doesn't replace your contract. It reads it — every operative term, every clause, every notice provision — and turns each one into a row the system can act on. From that moment forward, every event on the project routes through the term that governs it.

The Bridge

Every operative term gets classified across five coordinates: tier (legal weight), clause anchor (governing OpenDev rule), primary ledger (where the record lives), affected sections (where the ripple goes), and responsible actor (who acted, and whether that action binds).

That classification is what makes the contract computable. It's the difference between a PDF and a working model.

The Gate

A change order submitted is not a change order approved. A pay app submitted is not a pay app paid. A force majeure declared is not a force majeure accepted. Until the gate satisfies — every party in sequence, every deadline observed — the event is visible but it does not change what anyone owes anyone else.

When a deadline passes without action, the system escalates automatically. The audit trail shows exactly who was late and by how many days. Nobody rewrites that history.
